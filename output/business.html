Porsche GT3 Driver Asks for Car Back After 127-MPH Police Chase

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

If you ever find yourself running from the cops in a high-performance German sports car, it is probably best not to ask for the vehicle back once you are finally in handcuffs. A 21-year-old driver in Everett, Washington, learned this lesson the hard way after leading police on a wild, high-speed pursuit. The driver, identified as Tyger S. Yong, was behind the wheel of a bright blue 2014 Porsche 911 GT3 when a Snohomish County sheriff’s deputy clocked him traveling at a blistering 127 mph near Interstate 5.

Rather than pulling over, the driver allegedly attempted to outrun law enforcement. While we frequently see stories regarding teens fleeing police in stolen muscle cars, doing so in a highly recognizable, track-focused Porsche is a uniquely terrible idea. The chase eventually came to an abrupt halt when deputies successfully deployed spike strips, destroying both of the GT3’s passenger-side tires and forcing the expensive sports car to a complete stop in traffic.

All Fun and Games Until It Gets Real

According to a report from Road & Track, bodycam footage released by the sheriff’s office captured the immediate aftermath of the arrest, providing a brutally honest exchange between the suspect and the arresting deputy. When confronted about why he continued speeding, Yong reportedly claimed he was simply trying to get home. The deputy immediately dismissed the excuse, stating that the suspect was clearly trying to escape.

The situation became even more surreal when Yong asked if there was anything he could do to get his car back. The deputy delivered a deadpan, reality-checking response: “Not run from the police,” before explicitly warning the young driver that his prized sports car would likely end up belonging to the sheriff’s office. To make matters worse, Yong’s wife was riding in the passenger seat during the 127 mph pursuit, leading deputies to refer a domestic-violence-designated reckless endangerment allegation due to the severe risk of injury.

Porsche

Will He Get It Back?

Watching a beautifully engineered 911 GT3 get impounded is always a painful sight for any enthusiast. While the manufacturer is currently relying on high-margin models to boost its profits and continues finding ways to make its highly allocated track cars even more exclusive, this specific GT3 is permanently off the consumer market.

The Snohomish County Sheriff’s Office currently has official possession of the 2014 Porsche and is actively pursuing its legal seizure under state laws regarding vehicles used in felony eluding. Ultimately, this high-speed stunt serves as an incredibly expensive reminder that attempting to outrun a police radio will almost always result in losing your license, your freedom, and your dream car.

Rivian's $59,485 R2 Outsold the R1S and R1T Combined in Q3

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The R2

Rivian’s new midsize crossover performed well despite a high starting price. For now, the model starts at $59,485 for the R2 Performance with the Launch Package, but that hasn’t stopped customers from purchasing it anyway.

The rest of the lineup will follow in the near future. The Rivian R2 Performance, the sole trim since June this year, is the top-of-the-line variant, and it will eventually be joined by a mid-grade Premium and a base model that’ll only cost $46,485.

Rivian R2

Rivian

Outselling the R1S and R1T

Since June of this year, Rivian has noted that the demand for the R2 was pretty strong, and “reservation and conversion rates for the launch edition R2 remained higher in the quarter than our expectations,” said Rivian’s CEO, RJ Scaringe.

Now that the third quarter is finished, the company has since reported in its earnings call on October 29, 2026, that it outsold the bigger and more expensive R1S crossover and R1T pickup truck combined, becoming Rivian’s top-selling model in the quarter for the U.S. and Canada, while also beating out its sales for its electric delivery vans for Amazon.

The result can be attributed to how Rivian went about selling the R2. The brand raised its overall 2026 delivery guidance to between 67,000 and 70,000 vehicles. Previous delivery guidance was set between 65,000 and 70,000. Rivian also stated that it recently added a second shift at its Normal, Illinois, factory for the initial sales.

Rivian R2

Rivian

A Calculated Move

The R2 Performance launching first was no accident. Rivian stated that it’s “been able to very intentionally sequence the nature of launching with a high-price variant and then bringing in these other trims as our volume grows, such that in the end it supports a healthy, positive gross margin for the R2 business.”

High-end variants are usually where brands make the most money. With the R2 Performance, each sale returns a good margin back to Rivian for every car sold. When Rivian is ready to unleash its more affordable trims, it can potentially maintain the model’s sales momentum.

The R2 was well-received by the industry, and reviewers have praised it so far, along with a bit of controversy in tow. With 656 horsepower from a dual motor, all-wheel-drive, and a zero-to-sixty time of 3.6 seconds, it’s a rather exciting crossover. Couple that with a range of 330 miles, plus fast charging from 10% to 80% in under 30 minutes, and you have an EV that’s worthy of being a daily driver.

On top of that, the launch edition of the R2 comes with a lifetime Autonomy Plus driver-assistance subscription and a unique key fob in green, and an optional green exterior color and tow package.

Rivian R2

Rivian

Rivian Can’t Wait Too Long

With that being said, Rivian needs to strike while the iron is still hot and can’t wait too long to launch its more affordable trims. The R2 Performance starts at $59,485 and can go up from there.

Meanwhile, Rivian’s Premium trim, the mid-grade variant, will start at $55,485 (with shipping) and will come in “late 2026,” according to Rivian’s website. The midrange trim will come with 450 horsepower, all-wheel drive, and 330 miles of range. At the base model trim, the R2 Standard will start at $46,485 and come with 275 miles of range and be available as a rear-wheel-drive powertrain only. This model is expected to launch in summer 2027, according to Rivian.

Customers who want the base model will still be able to spec it out in case they want more capability. The long-range version will cost $3,500 more, but customers may also get long-range and all-wheel drive for $7,000.

The Ford Escape PHEV: America's Slowest-Selling Used Car

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Hybrids are having a moment on the used market, and the Ford Escape plug-in hybrid is sitting the whole thing out. In an analysis of over 1.5 million used vehicles aged one to five years, the shopping site iSeeCars found that electrified cars are moving quickly while gas cars languish. The Escape PHEV did the opposite. It ranked first on the firm's roster of the slowest-moving used cars in the country, the single hardest-to-move used vehicle out there.


How slow is slow? The Escape PHEV sits on the used market for 154.5 days. The average used car moves in 53.4 days, which means the Escape PHEV takes 2.9 times as long to find a buyer. That is not a rounding error. That is a car dealers would rather not stock.


The broader story favors electrification. Used EVs posted the biggest price increases of any drivetrain, with the average asking price climbing 9.8 percent even as the time to sell dropped 10.1 percent. They now move faster than both gas and hybrid models. Hybrids, meanwhile, have logged moderate gains in both price and selling speed, while gas cars have watched their selling times stretch out despite relatively stable prices.


So the tailwind is real, and the Escape PHEV is flying straight into it. Karl Brauer, an executive analyst at iSeeCars, framed the split bluntly. He said that, on average, electrified cars are outselling gas vehicles, but that some electrified models linger far longer on the lot than a typical used car does. The well-built ones are cashing in on high gas prices. The rest are not. As Brauer put it, even high fuel prices cannot save an unpopular electric car.


The Escape PHEV was not alone on the slowest-selling list. Several other hybrids and EVs showed up there too, which tells you the electrified surge is uneven. Buyers are rewarding specific models, not drivetrains. The Escape PHEV simply landed on the wrong side of that sorting.

A used car this sticky usually has a reason, and the Escape PHEV's reason is on file with the federal government. Across seven model years, there are 10 recall entries in the NHTSA database, plus 129 owner complaints. That is a thick folder for a compact SUV.


The complaints cluster where you would least want them on a plug-in hybrid. The electrical system is the most-complained-about component with 51 complaints, followed closely by the engine at 46. After that, the numbers fall off: 16 for unknown or other components, 15 for the power train, 11 for the fuel and propulsion system, 7 for the structure, 5 for forward collision avoidance, and 4 for the service brakes. Electrical and engine trouble on an electrified drivetrain is exactly the kind of thing that makes a shopper pause.


The problems are not spread evenly across the years, either. The 2022 model year alone carries 80 owner complaints and 2 recalls, making it the clear outlier. By contrast, the 2023 PHEV has 13 complaints and 2 recalls, the 2024 has 8 complaints and 2 recalls, the 2025 has 4 complaints and 1 recall, and the 2026 shows just 1 complaint and 1 recall. The early cars did the heavy lifting on complaints. The 2021 PHEV logged 22 complaints against a single recall, and even the low-volume 2020 has 1 recall and 1 complaint.


The one thing the Escape PHEV does not lack is a crash score. Every model year with a rating on file, 2022, 2023, 2024, and 2025, earned a 5.0-star overall NCAP rating. The 2020, 2021, and 2026 cars have no overall NCAP rating on file. Safety is not the problem here. Buyer confidence is.

The PHEV's troubles sit inside a larger pattern for the fourth-generation Escape, which covers model years 2020 through 2024 under the CX482 code. It is a comfortable, efficient compact SUV that happens to carry among the worst recall records in its segment. That is a hard sentence to put on a window sticker.


The generation offered a wide spread of powertrains: a 181-hp 1.5L EcoBoost three-cylinder, a 250-hp 2.0L EcoBoost, a 200-hp 2.5L hybrid, and the 221-hp 2.5L PHEV. Each electrified version brought its own baggage. The 1.5L carries a cracked-fuel-injector fire risk tied to a recall that runs across nearly the whole production span. Both the 2.5L hybrid and the plug-in version, in turn, come with engine-failure and battery fire-risk recalls of their own. The early cars in particular stack up powertrain and electronics complaints, which lines up with what the NHTSA tally shows for 2020 through 2022.


None of this makes the Escape a lost cause. A fourth-gen Escape with every recall closed out is considered a solid family hauler. The catch is the word "closed." Open recalls are what turn a good compact SUV into a 154-day lot ornament.

If you still want one, the buying math is straightforward. The 2023 and 2024 model years are the best of the generation, because the facelift sidesteps most of the first-year problems. The 2020 model year is the one to avoid, since it was the first year of the generation and carries the most complaints and recalls. A 2020 example with unresolved recalls and a patchy dealer history is a hard pass.


By engine, the 2.0L EcoBoost is the cleanest choice, and the hybrid is the value play once you have confirmed its recalls, while the 1.5L cars from 2020 through 2022 should be avoided unless the fuel-injector recall is confirmed closed against the VIN. For the PHEV specifically, the rule is strict: pass on it no matter the model year unless the paperwork shows every high-voltage battery recall closed.


That verification step is the whole ballgame. Before you buy, check the VIN against the NHTSA recall lookup at nhtsa.gov/recalls. For a 1.5L car, confirm the fuel-injector recall is closed. For a hybrid, confirm the 2.5L engine-failure recall inspection is done. For a PHEV, confirm every high-voltage battery recall is completed. A clean recall report is what separates a bargain from the reason this car is the slowest-selling used vehicle in America.


The nameplate's future is in flux. Escape production ended late last year, and Ford expects the model to return at some point, possibly riding on the company's Universal Electric Vehicle platform as the second model on that architecture. Whether that revival fixes the trust problem is an open question, but it does mean today's used cars are the last of an era.

The irony is that earlier versions of the Escape still sell well used. It is this generation, and the PHEV in particular, that buyers are steering around. The lesson is the one Brauer kept circling: electrification sells, but only when the car behind it earns the buyer's confidence. The Escape PHEV has the drivetrain the market wants and a recall record the market cannot ignore.


Your move as a shopper is simple. Decide which engine fits your use, target a 2023 or 2024 car, and treat the NHTSA recall lookup as a hard gate rather than a formality. Do that, and the slowest-selling used car in America can become a comfortable, efficient compact SUV at a price nobody else wants to pay. Skip it, and you inherit the exact problem that kept the car on the lot for 154 days.

In Memoriam: The Audi A8, 1994-2026

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

In just a few weeks the production of Audi’s flagship sedan will draw to a close. After 32 years in production it will be replaced by a new flagship of an expected shape, but Audi is hesitant to draw the direct connection between the models itself. Let’s take a look at three decades and five generations of Audi’s most prestigious four-door as we say goodbye to the A8.


Audi’s first full-size luxury sedan was the A8’s predecessor, the V8 Quattro. It would’ve been called an A8, but Audi didn’t have its A# nomenclature just yet. Riding on the D1 platform (an enlarged version of the 100/5000 chassis), the V8 Quattro was the first Audi with an automatic transmission and full-time four-wheel drive, and the first Audi with a V8. It was built at the Neckarsulm factory that built the NSU Ro 80, and would go on to build every A8 generation.


Interestingly, the V8 Quattro was available with a smaller 3.6-liter V8, or the 4.2-liter most people associate with Audi of the time. It was also available as a long-wheelbase model with 13 inches of additional length. Produced from 1988 to 1993, the V8 Quattro was not much of a sales success but mixed the basic ingredients that would form the A8.

The first A8 (D2 platform) that debuted as a 1995 model was the realization of an expensive project that began in 1982. Ferdinand Piëch signed an agreement with ALCOA of America to develop a lightweight aluminum-intensive platform that would save hundreds of pounds over traditional frame construction. Known as the Audi Space Frame, the A8 concept was first shown at the 1993 Frankfurt Motor Show.


The D2 didn’t arrive in America until 1997, when a 3.7-liter V8 and front-wheel drive were offered for a short time. Yours truly owned a D2 A8L with the 4.2-liter V8, in Melange Metallic over beige. With 135k miles it was supremely comfortable, had a beautiful interior, regularly returned 24 mpg, leaked AC condensation water onto the floor, and even in 2010 had parts availability issues. On its second or third owner since me, that A8 is still on the road in Indiana and has well over 300,000 miles. 

In 2003, the D3 generation arrived, and was no longer the only VW Group vehicle to use its platform. D3 platform buddies included the Bentley Flying Spur and Volkswagen Phaeton as VAG expanded its full-size sedan offerings into new brands. However those cars used steel body shells rather than the Audi’s aluminum. Again available in two wheelbases, A8 offered V6, V8, V10, and W12 power. Arguably the height of the A8 as a concept, the D3 would remain on offer through the 2010 model year.

Simultaneously in 2010 the “D4” A8 arrived, the model’s third generation. No longer on a dedicated luxury sedan platform, the A8 was switched to the VW MLB platform and was directly related to (and a larger version of) the A6 and A7. If one could pick a point where things headed downhill, it was here. 

Four-cylinder engines were available for the first time, in addition to the other expected cylinder configurations. Top tier was a quite large 6.3-liter W12. Production expanded in 2013 to include a Russian factory, so the A8 was no longer a single-source vehicle.

In 2018, the D5 A8 arrived as the model’s fourth and final iteration. It used the MLB Evo platform for C- and D-segment cars shared with the Audi A6, A7, Q7, Volkswagen Touareg, and Porsche Cayenne among others. Four- and 12-cylinder power was gone, as engine options were limited to V6 and V8 configurations.

For the first time there were three wheelbases available: standard, long, and extra long for the Chinese market. The latter saw an historical model name resurrected from the dead, as a Horch trim was exclusively sold in China. With a wheelbase of 128.3 inches and overall length of 213.5 inches, the Horch was 10 inches longer than a standard A8. 

Updated for the 2022 model year, it was around this time Audi began to mumble about the A8’s replacement. The company debuted its Grandsphere Concept that previewed an electric version of the A8 in early 2023. The concept was slated to arrive as a replacement for the more traditional A8 in 2024.

Reports indicated the new model would be called A8 e-tron. However the global market’s take rate on EVs was slower than anticipated, and Audi backed off their “all-EV by 2033” claims. Models like the Q8 E-tron have come and gone, while the traditional A8 soldiered on without a named replacement. 


That was until January 2026 when Audi quietly stopped taking orders for the A8 in Germany. Production will wind down at the end of this year, with a singular trim (3.0T V6, mild-hybrid) available in the US for 2027. It’s solely available in standard wheelbase format across six exterior colors. Never fear, as Audi has a new large four-door replacement available!


While the company will not directly draw the line between the outgoing A8 and its new flagship, the crown passes obviously to the Q9 CUV. Riding on the latest version of the MLB known as Premium Platform Combustion (shared with A5, A6, and Q7), the Q9 has a 123.6-inch wheelbase and 209.1-inch overall length, putting it squarely in the same size category as the outgoing A8.

One might assume that falling sales were to blame for the A8’s demise. On the contrary, while in 2003 the model managed 21,748 sales, in 2013 it sold 39,757 examples, and in 2023 totals were 20,293. Throughout the 2010s the A8 sold better than it ever had in its D2 or D3 generation, barring a single dip in 2009 because of the global recession. 

Rather it seems the A8 was a victim of Audi’s product planning flub: An ambitious idea to replace ICE vehicles with e-tron EVs that didn’t pan out. There was no Plan B at Audi for A8. It simply led to a final generation that stretched too many years without a real successor. 

Now cancelled, the A8 is replaced with a crossover as Audi slims their sedan range to almost nothing (their best-selling sedan the A4 died last year). It puts Audi in the interesting position of being the only member of the German Three to lack a range-topping luxury sedan. BMW and Mercedes will gladly accept A8 customers at any time. The latter brand will even sell you a sedan with 12 cylinders. Rest in peace, A8.

Porsche Rules Out U.S. Plant Even as Audi Bets $4 Billion

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Audi decided it wants a $4 billion plant in the United States. Porsche, which shares a corporate parent, a platform or two, and even a factory with its sister brand, wants no part of it.


"As things stand, should an Audi plant be built in the U.S., we do not plan to relocate Porsche production there," financial chief Jochen Breckner told analysts on an earnings call. The question was fair game, given that Audi and Porsche already build the Q7, Q8, and the related Cayenne under one roof in Slovakia.


Breckner was blunt about why. "The business case would not be advantageous for us, given the complexity of our product range, our lower production volumes and the additional investment that would be required," he said.


That is the honest version. Audi plays a volume game dressed up as a premium one. Porsche plays a margin game, where the real money lives in options, packages, pricier models, and the occasional extreme one-off. Its buyers are chasing performance and a badge, not a sticker price, which means the 15 percent tariff Trump slapped on imported cars last year stings less for Porsche than it would for a brand fighting over every dollar.


Then there is the capital question. Porsche has been struggling to post a meaningful profit, though it did manage to grow third-quarter earnings despite falling sales. A multibillion-dollar American factory is not in the budget, and by Porsche's own logic, not in the plan either.

Porsche's turnaround strategy, dubbed Sportwagenschmiede 35, leans hard into exclusivity. Building higher-volume models in Slovakia and Malaysia already chips at the "made in Germany" expectation baked into the brand. A U.S. line would chip harder.


There is one loose thread. A U.S. official said Audi's American plans would include the Q7 and Q8, which share a factory with the Cayenne. That technically cracks the door open for U.S.-built Porsches. For now, Porsche is keeping it shut.
