In Memoriam: The Audi A8, 1994-2026

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

In just a few weeks the production of Audi’s flagship sedan will draw to a close. After 32 years in production it will be replaced by a new flagship of an expected shape, but Audi is hesitant to draw the direct connection between the models itself. Let’s take a look at three decades and five generations of Audi’s most prestigious four-door as we say goodbye to the A8.


Audi’s first full-size luxury sedan was the A8’s predecessor, the V8 Quattro. It would’ve been called an A8, but Audi didn’t have its A# nomenclature just yet. Riding on the D1 platform (an enlarged version of the 100/5000 chassis), the V8 Quattro was the first Audi with an automatic transmission and full-time four-wheel drive, and the first Audi with a V8. It was built at the Neckarsulm factory that built the NSU Ro 80, and would go on to build every A8 generation.


Interestingly, the V8 Quattro was available with a smaller 3.6-liter V8, or the 4.2-liter most people associate with Audi of the time. It was also available as a long-wheelbase model with 13 inches of additional length. Produced from 1988 to 1993, the V8 Quattro was not much of a sales success but mixed the basic ingredients that would form the A8.

The first A8 (D2 platform) that debuted as a 1995 model was the realization of an expensive project that began in 1982. Ferdinand Piëch signed an agreement with ALCOA of America to develop a lightweight aluminum-intensive platform that would save hundreds of pounds over traditional frame construction. Known as the Audi Space Frame, the A8 concept was first shown at the 1993 Frankfurt Motor Show.


The D2 didn’t arrive in America until 1997, when a 3.7-liter V8 and front-wheel drive were offered for a short time. Yours truly owned a D2 A8L with the 4.2-liter V8, in Melange Metallic over beige. With 135k miles it was supremely comfortable, had a beautiful interior, regularly returned 24 mpg, leaked AC condensation water onto the floor, and even in 2010 had parts availability issues. On its second or third owner since me, that A8 is still on the road in Indiana and has well over 300,000 miles. 

In 2003, the D3 generation arrived, and was no longer the only VW Group vehicle to use its platform. D3 platform buddies included the Bentley Flying Spur and Volkswagen Phaeton as VAG expanded its full-size sedan offerings into new brands. However those cars used steel body shells rather than the Audi’s aluminum. Again available in two wheelbases, A8 offered V6, V8, V10, and W12 power. Arguably the height of the A8 as a concept, the D3 would remain on offer through the 2010 model year.

Simultaneously in 2010 the “D4” A8 arrived, the model’s third generation. No longer on a dedicated luxury sedan platform, the A8 was switched to the VW MLB platform and was directly related to (and a larger version of) the A6 and A7. If one could pick a point where things headed downhill, it was here. 

Four-cylinder engines were available for the first time, in addition to the other expected cylinder configurations. Top tier was a quite large 6.3-liter W12. Production expanded in 2013 to include a Russian factory, so the A8 was no longer a single-source vehicle.

In 2018, the D5 A8 arrived as the model’s fourth and final iteration. It used the MLB Evo platform for C- and D-segment cars shared with the Audi A6, A7, Q7, Volkswagen Touareg, and Porsche Cayenne among others. Four- and 12-cylinder power was gone, as engine options were limited to V6 and V8 configurations.

For the first time there were three wheelbases available: standard, long, and extra long for the Chinese market. The latter saw an historical model name resurrected from the dead, as a Horch trim was exclusively sold in China. With a wheelbase of 128.3 inches and overall length of 213.5 inches, the Horch was 10 inches longer than a standard A8. 

Updated for the 2022 model year, it was around this time Audi began to mumble about the A8’s replacement. The company debuted its Grandsphere Concept that previewed an electric version of the A8 in early 2023. The concept was slated to arrive as a replacement for the more traditional A8 in 2024.

Reports indicated the new model would be called A8 e-tron. However the global market’s take rate on EVs was slower than anticipated, and Audi backed off their “all-EV by 2033” claims. Models like the Q8 E-tron have come and gone, while the traditional A8 soldiered on without a named replacement. 


That was until January 2026 when Audi quietly stopped taking orders for the A8 in Germany. Production will wind down at the end of this year, with a singular trim (3.0T V6, mild-hybrid) available in the US for 2027. It’s solely available in standard wheelbase format across six exterior colors. Never fear, as Audi has a new large four-door replacement available!


While the company will not directly draw the line between the outgoing A8 and its new flagship, the crown passes obviously to the Q9 CUV. Riding on the latest version of the MLB known as Premium Platform Combustion (shared with A5, A6, and Q7), the Q9 has a 123.6-inch wheelbase and 209.1-inch overall length, putting it squarely in the same size category as the outgoing A8.

One might assume that falling sales were to blame for the A8’s demise. On the contrary, while in 2003 the model managed 21,748 sales, in 2013 it sold 39,757 examples, and in 2023 totals were 20,293. Throughout the 2010s the A8 sold better than it ever had in its D2 or D3 generation, barring a single dip in 2009 because of the global recession. 

Rather it seems the A8 was a victim of Audi’s product planning flub: An ambitious idea to replace ICE vehicles with e-tron EVs that didn’t pan out. There was no Plan B at Audi for A8. It simply led to a final generation that stretched too many years without a real successor. 

Now cancelled, the A8 is replaced with a crossover as Audi slims their sedan range to almost nothing (their best-selling sedan the A4 died last year). It puts Audi in the interesting position of being the only member of the German Three to lack a range-topping luxury sedan. BMW and Mercedes will gladly accept A8 customers at any time. The latter brand will even sell you a sedan with 12 cylinders. Rest in peace, A8.

Toyota Unveils Five Rugged Woodland Concepts, Including A Lifted Sienna

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Toyota is coming to this year’s SEMA Show with something for everyone. Earlier this week, it unveiled the racy Crown Signia GT-S Concept, a much sportier take on the hybrid crossover for the street. The automaker has now taken the covers off something entirely different: A family of five Woodland concepts. Unlike the Crown Signia GT-S, the Woodland concepts reimagine a lineup of rugged, more capable Toyotas that can tackle a dirt road or campsite trail as effortlessly as the daily commute.

Five Concepts, One Family Look

Toyota Woodland Concepts

Toyota

Toyota chose the following five models as the basis for the Woodland concepts:

  • Toyota RAV4
  • Toyota Grand Highlander
  • Toyota Sienna
  • Toyota bZ
  • Toyota Crown Signia

Toyota RAV4 Woodland Concept

Toyota

View the 5 images of this gallery on the original article

All models are finished in striking Horizon Blue paint with contrasting black hoods/roofs. The blue shade was used on classic Toyota models in the 1960s, including the Stout, Crown, and Corona. CALTY Design Research created a visual and functional identity for all five concepts, which are said to be built around exploration without compromising usability. They all feature lift kits, satin-black wheels wrapped in all-terrain tires, roof racks, cross bars, and aggressive over-fenders.

My pick for the coolest, most unexpected model in the quintet has to be the Sienna Woodland. It couldn’t be further removed from the conservative hybrid minivan that is more often seen at the mall or offloading kids at schools across the country. While most Toyota SUVs have rugged, off-road trims, the Sienna does not, but what could be better than an eight-seater van that can handle some mild off-roading?

Rugged Performance, Regardless of The Powertrain

Toyota Grand Highlander Woodland Concept

Toyota

View the 3 images of this gallery on the original article

With the Woodland concepts, Toyota also aims to show that capability need not be limited to one powertrain type. The models here include hybrid, plug-in hybrid, and battery-electric options. 

While no specifications were shared, we can get a sense of what’s possible from the production Woodland models Toyota already sells. For instance, the bZ Woodland electric SUV has standard all-wheel drive and a handy 8.4 inches of ground clearance. It also features X-Mode, which optimizes power delivery and brake control, improving traction across loose surfaces. Another X-Mode function is Grip Control, a system that modulates power delivery in tough conditions, while reacting to different surface conditions. The RAV4 Woodland also has more ground clearance than standard trims and comes with extras like all-terrain tires and a tow hitch.

Toyota Will Thrill Off-Road Fans At SEMA

Toyota RAV4 Woodland Concept

Toyota

View the 3 images of this gallery on the original article

All in all, it’s shaping up to be an exciting SEMA Show for Toyota fans, especially those into off-roading. Besides the Crown Signia GTS and Woodland concepts, the automaker already showed off Tacoma and Tundra pickups converted into snow machines—probably the wildest creations yet from the Japanese brand. While those snow machines are unlikely to influence production models, the new Woodland concepts are much likelier to inspire a Toyota that could end up on the showroom floor. With Honda dialing up the intensity for its TrailSport lineup, Toyota could do the same with the Woodland range.

These concepts and other models will be showcased at the event from November 3, 2026, with public access from November 6. The event takes place at the Las Vegas Convention Center.
