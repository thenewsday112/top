McLaren Reveals Name and Power of Its Next Supercar as the 750S Faces the Axe

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Automotive  |  Read Source →

The Next Chapter Has a Name

McLaren has confirmed that a new supercar called the McL 820 will debut in 2027. Like many McLaren models, the number represents its power output, which, in this case, is 820 PS or 809 horsepower. What the press release didn’t confirm is whether the McL 820 will replace the brand’s flagship series-production model, the 750S.

However, that will likely be the case, which makes sense given that the latter is nearing the end of production and is taking final orders. The 750S is a heavily updated version of the 720S, which debuted in 2017 as the successor to the 650S. The Artura, on the other hand, isn’t expected to be discontinued until 2028. Still, with the British marque yet to confirm which model the new supercar will replace, nothing is set in stone.

McLaren

Building Its Own Muscle

Whatever the case may be, the McL 820 could benefit from McLaren’s plans to develop and manufacture engines and transmissions in-house under its expansion strategy. Historically, the automaker relied on British engineering company Ricardo to manufacture engines for its road cars, including the twin-turbo V8s used in the 750S lineage.

With McLaren developing new hybrid powertrains, the McL 820 could feature some form of electrification. Its 809 hp is up 69 hp over the 750S and 32 hp over the 788HS, a limited-edition model marking the end of the outgoing supercar’s production run.

McLaren has already shown its capabilities in the hybrid segment with the W1 hypercar, which combines a 4.0-liter twin-turbo V8 and an electric motor to produce 1,258 horsepower. Still, the 809-hp output puts the new supercar in the same ballpark as the Ferrari 296 GTB with its 819-hp hybrid V6, Ferrari 12Cilindri with its 819-hp NA V12, and Aston Martin Vanquish with its 824-hp twin-turbo V12.

McLaren

A Bigger Garage for McLaren

The McL 820 will be one of the new models joining the lineup this decade, alongside a potential 2+2 grand tourer and a confirmed SUV. The latter will let McLaren tap into the lucrative SUV segment, which has sustained demand, as seen with other supercar brands like Lamborghini and Aston Martin joining the fray with the Urus and DBX, respectively. What isn’t coming, at least anytime soon, is an all-electric model, something Ferrari has already embraced with the Luce.

In the U.S., McLaren is reportedly considering expanding its dealer network by up to 40% over the next two years amid growing demand stateside. The expansion could help the brand reach more buyers as it rolls out its next generation of supercars and enters new segments.

Rivian's Amazon Van Gets 710-HP AWD, 30% More Range

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Automotive  |  Read Source →

Rivian's commercial van has been coasting on a single trick for years: it's the EV that Amazon bought by the tens of thousands, which, according to Cox Automotive, was enough to make it America's best-selling electric van. Now the company has handed it some actual range and power, and the reasoning is refreshingly blunt.



The Rivian Commercial Van (RCV), which also goes by Electric Delivery Van (EDV), can now be ordered with a larger battery pack that extends range by roughly 30% over the standard version. Per EPA filings, the Large pack carries a usable capacity of 129 kWh, well above the Standard pack's 90 kWh, and it's restricted to the long-wheelbase variants.


Per the EPA certification filing, the front-wheel-drive EDV 700 is good for about 220 miles, a 37% jump over the standard-range model. Rivian hasn't published final range numbers for the new configurations.


Both the old and new packs use lithium iron phosphate (LFP) cells.


The more eye-catching addition is a dual-motor, all-wheel-drive setup. It pairs two Enduro motors for a combined 710 horsepower (530 kilowatts), and like the big battery, it's exclusive to the 700 body version. Add the Large pack and AWD together, and range dips to about 200 miles, per the EPA certification filing.

Rivian framed the updates around coverage, not bragging rights. "In addition to the EDV standard pack variant already on the road, in early October we began delivering new variants that are specifically designed to expand route coverage into more rural networks, including an AWD configuration as well as a larger battery pack that offers a 30% increase in range," the company said in its third-quarter earnings report.


The extra hardware carries a weight penalty. Per Rivian's spec sheet, the 129 kWh pack adds roughly 1,000 pounds, and the rear motor another 450. According to Rivian, a fully loaded long-wheelbase van now carries a curb weight of 8,677 pounds against a 10,000-pound GVWR.


Charging stays the same: a CCS1 port good for up to 100 kW DC and 11 kW AC, with NACS adapters offered as an opt-out option, Rivian said.

The hardware still sells. Cox Automotive data shows Rivian delivered 15,685 EDVs and RCVs over the first nine months of the year, keeping it well ahead of every rival in the electric commercial van race.

Ford's Escape Site Teases a 2029 EV Comeback

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Automotive  |  Read Source →

Dead nameplates don't usually leave the door cracked open. The Ford Escape, whose production wrapped at the Louisville Assembly plant last December, has done exactly that.


When Ford discontinues a model, it typically converts the official landing page into a farewell. The Edge, for instance, gets the blunt treatment: its site declares "the end of the road." The Escape got softer language.


"Ford Escape is tapping the brakes," reads the banner on the official Escape page. Below it: "The Ford Escape SUV may be taking a breather for now, but the rest of our dynamic vehicle lineup is always going strong. Discover the perfect fit for the next phase of your Ford journey."


That is not the vocabulary of a dead model. "Taking a breather" and "for now" are doing a lot of work for a crossover that is no longer in production.


Whatever the copywriters are hinting at, the current Escape is effectively sold out. New dealer gross stock sat at roughly 500 units at the end of September, and the very last Escape built found a buyer a few weeks before that. The small, affordable crossover slot in Ford's lineup is empty.

The teasing isn't idle. The Escape is returning as a compact all-electric crossover built on the new Universal Electric Vehicle (UEV) platform. The reborn Escape carries the internal designation U833, is sized similarly to the outgoing model, and is set to begin production at the Louisville plant in 2029.


That makes it the second UEV-platform vehicle, coming after the Ford Fathom, which is due to begin building at the same plant sometime in 2027.


So the website coyness lines up with the plan. Ford knows the Escape name is coming back, it just won't be back for a while, and it won't run on gasoline. For buyers who wanted a cheap gas crossover in the meantime, that 2029 date is a long breather indeed.
