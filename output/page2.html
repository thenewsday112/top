Waymo Is Turning Toyota's Electric SUV Into A Driverless Robotaxi

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Toyota and Waymo announced a strategic collaboration 18 months ago, and the two companies have now outlined the next phase of their plan to work together on autonomous robotaxis. The new robotaxis are set to be validated and tested in Japan next year, using the Toyota bZ4X electric crossover (currently known as the Toyota bZ in the United States). The collaboration will mark the first domestically manufactured fully autonomous ride-hailing service in Japan, before the robotaxis are deployed in other major markets.

Related: Toyota and Waymo Strike Partnership to Bring Robotaxi Tech to Personal Cars

Initial Testing and Validation Begin in 2027

Toyota bZ4X robotaxi

Toyota

Next year, Toyota and Waymo will conduct testing and validation of the autonomous Toyota bZ4X in Japan. Toyota is a global manufacturing powerhouse, and combining this with Waymo’s expertise in autonomous vehicles could prove to be a formidable combination. The specialized Toyota SUVs will be built at the automaker’s Takaoka plant in Toyota City, Japan. Rather than build the vehicle base and have Waymo install its hardware at another location, everything will take place under one roof.

Toyota’s goal is not only to provide a more convenient means of transportation, especially for those with mobility difficulties, but also to reduce accidents that involve human error. The companies will still work towards enabling autonomous technology for personally-owned Toyota vehicles.

“In areas where Waymo operates, we’ve demonstrated that we are up to 20x safer than human drivers when it comes to serious crash injuries, and our partnership with Toyota will help us bring the safety and convenience of autonomous driving to more people around the world, including key international markets like Japan,” said Dmitri Dolgov, co-CEO of Waymo.

Toyota bZ4X Prepares For New Role

Toyota bZ4X robotaxi

Toyota

The Toyota bZ4X never got off to the greatest start in the U.S., but the renamed bZ has flourished this year after a series of updates were introduced for the 2026 model year; sales are up by 103.4% in the U.S. this year so far.

The autonomous bZ4X will receive a number of enhancements as it transitions into its role as a robotaxi. According to Toyota, it has been designed for greater comfort and accessibility, with a focus on rear legroom. It will also come with features not found on the regular bZ, such as automatic doors. A power liftgate and special ambient lighting will make ingress/egress more convenient for passengers. Above all, it will adopt Waymo’s leading self-driving tech, which the autonomous company claims has a 93% satisfaction rate.

“Safety technology makes the biggest difference when it reaches as many people as possible,” said Hiroki Nakajima, member of the board of directors and executive vice president of Toyota. “This collaboration will help scale that technology and bring it to customers in Japan and beyond through Waymo’s ride-hailing service, and in parallel, Toyota will continue to explore how integrating vehicles with traffic infrastructure can make those roads safer still.”

Planned Market Rollout

Toyota bZ4X robotaxi

Toyota

Once testing and validation have been completed in Japan, the autonomous Toyota bZ4X fleet is expected to roll out there, before expanding to the United Kingdom, so right-hand-drive markets are being prioritized initially. Other Waymo-serviced markets are expected to come on board in the coming years, but Toyota did not specifically mention when customers in the United States can expect to make use of these self-driving Toyotas.

For most commuters, the most exciting aspect of the Waymo and Toyota partnership will be the planned introduction of autonomous tech into personally-owned vehicles. The idea of a reliable Toyota with a possible Tesla-rivaling self-driving system will be of great interest to many buyers. Lessons learned from the bZ4X robotaxi project are likely to accelerate this possibility in the years ahead.

Lamborghini Temerario Gets a Wild Two-Tone Makeover in Five Colors

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

A Two-Tone Treatment for the Baby Lambo

Lamborghini clearly wants the Temerario to stand out, and this time, it’s all about the paint job. Enter the Temerario Tettonero Capsule – a special edition that cranks up the two-tone look on this hybrid supercar.

Dreamed up by Lamborghini Centro Stile, this collection riffs on the capsule idea first seen on the Urus SE at Milan Design Week 2026. That one gave the Super SUV a taste of Tettonero, but now the same bold design lands on something much lower, sleeker, and a lot more focused on speed.

The main party trick here is the clash between your chosen paint and the deep Nero Noctis black accents. Pick from five wild colors: Giallo Inti, Arancio Xanto, Blu Cepheus, Verde Scandal, or Viola Pasifae. Giallo Inti is the launch star, but the rest let you dial in your own flavor.

Nero Noctis covers the hood, roof, and a bunch of custom exterior bits. The blacked-out look keeps going on the front splitter, rear diffuser, badge, and engine surround. Even the bumpers, mirrors, and wheels get the High Gloss Black treatment, just to make sure nobody misses the two-tone vibe.

Lamborghini

Details Continue Inside the Cabin

But the wild paint job is just the start. Lamborghini has snuck a few exclusive touches into the cabin too, even if they’re keeping most of them under wraps for now. The idea is to tie the whole look together without messing too much with the regular interior.

Inside, you get Nero Ade upholstery with Grigio Octans stitching for contrast. The Q-citura seats rock a hexagonal pattern, and you can even match the stitching to your chosen paint. It’s a neat way to link the outside and inside, especially if you’re the type who wants everything color-coordinated.

You also get embroidered Lamborghini logos on the headrests, Tettonero script on the doors, and a stitched two-tone outline of the rear end on the bulkhead. Each car gets a Matt Carbon plaque on the driver’s side, just to remind you it’s part of the special club.

Lamborghini

Same Electrified V8 Underneath

Naturally, all this flash doesn’t mess with what’s under the skin. The Temerario still packs the new 4.0-liter twin-turbo V8 and three electric motors beneath its custom suit.

That hybrid setup cranks out a combined 907 horsepower, and the V8 can scream all the way to 10,000 rpm. So you still get the wild performance you expect from a modern Lamborghini, just with a side of electrification.

Lamborghini hasn’t mentioned any mechanical tweaks for the Tettonero, so this one’s all about style points, not extra speed.
